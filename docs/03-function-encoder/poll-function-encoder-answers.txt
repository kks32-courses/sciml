Answers to poll-function-encoder.txt, in order, with the slide behind each.

1. A least-squares solve for k coefficients, with the basis fixed.
   The basis networks are frozen after offline training.
   The coefficients solve (Phi^T Phi + lambda I) alpha = Phi^T y, a k by k system, with no gradient step and no encoder network ("Online Inference: Compute Coefficients").

2. Stays at the level seen inside the training range, because least squares projects onto the same span.
   Least squares is the orthogonal projection onto the span of the basis, and projection is linear.
   A quadratic with large coefficients is a scaled combination of training quadratics, so its relative error equals the type 1 error.
   An autoencoder's decoder only returns functions near the training set, which is why it fails here ("Function Encoder: Transfer Learning", type 2).

3. The best quadratic approximation to the cubic in the least-squares sense.
   A cubic lies outside the span of the quadratics, which is type 3 transfer.
   The projection returns the closest element of the span, the best quadratic fit.
   The universal approximation theorem promises enough basis functions exist to represent any function, and says nothing about three functions trained on quadratics ("Function Encoder: Transfer Learning", type 3).

4. The m kernel terms are combinations of the k basis functions, so the predictor collapses to k coefficients.
   The dual predictor sum_l beta_l k(x, x_l) equals phi(x)^T Phi^T beta, a combination of the k basis functions.
   Evaluation costs O(k) in the primal against O(m) in the dual ("Offline training", inference cost O(k) against O(m)).
   The learned kernel is not stationary, and a trained Gram matrix is close to the identity only when a penalty asks for it.

5. The operator restricted to the training family has low effective rank in those bases.
   A finite matrix always has finitely many singular values, so their decay cannot show compactness of an operator on the whole function space.
   The singular values also change under any rescaling of the learned bases.
   For the derivative on cubics the rank is at most 3 whatever the matrix size, and the derivative operator itself is unbounded ("B2B linear example").
