Answers to poll-function-encoder-2.txt, in order, with the notebook section behind each.

1. Span the same subspace as the first k POD modes of the family, for every k.
   The best k-dimensional subspaces of the family are the POD subspaces, and they are nested, so one basis can reach the best error at every k at once.
   The loss does not change under a triangular change of basis within each prefix, so it fixes the spans and puts no condition on the Gram matrix.
   A standard basis is trained only through the span of all eight functions, and its four top-ranked functions give test MSE 4.55e-2 against 9.59e-3 for the Matryoshka basis and 3.03e-3 for the first four POD modes (03-function-encoder.ipynb, Part 6, Nested Bases with a Matryoshka Loss).

2. Bounds the movement by a factor set by lambda for any basis, and shrinks the coefficient vector toward zero.
   With lambda > 0 the factor max_i sigma_i / (sigma_i^2 + lambda) is at most 1/(2 sqrt(lambda)), whatever the singular values of Phi are.
   With sigma_min = 6.1e-5 the coefficient bound falls from 16283.4 at lambda = 0 to 4.5 at lambda = 1e-2, and the measured worst case falls from 3892.2 to 3.9.
   The noise still moves the fit at every lambda, with measured coefficient amplification 0.4 at lambda = 1.
   The ridge also shrinks the fit, which at lambda = 1e-2 stays below the peak of the new function, and the noise bound does not measure this bias (03-function-encoder.ipynb, Part 8, Guarantees at Inference Time).

3. Widens the gap term, so the bound can loosen even as the empirical risk falls.
   The gap term is of order Y^2 R^2 k / (lambda sqrt(m)), so it grows with the number k of basis functions and shrinks with the number m of samples.
   The approximation quality of the basis enters through the empirical risk, so a richer basis does not shrink the gap.
   In the kernels notebook a 12-function basis fitted to 20 noisy samples wiggles between the samples with RMSE 0.289, and the same basis reaches 0.019 with 80 samples.
   The notebook attributes the wiggles to basis directions the vehicle family does not need, which are free to fit the noise (03-function-encoder.ipynb, Part 8, Guarantees at Inference Time, and 03-fe-kernels.ipynb, Section 9, What the two predictors are made of).

4. Bounded and not compact, because its singular values do not tend to zero.
   Every n by n matrix is compact, so the test reads how the singular values behave as n grows, and a compact operator needs sigma_k -> 0.
   The identity keeps n singular values at 1 on every grid, while the Poisson solve keeps 10 singular values above 0.01 sigma_1 on every grid from n = 25 to n = 400.
   The largest singular value of the identity stays at 1.0000 under refinement, so the identity is bounded, while the largest singular value of the derivative grows from 25.8 to 401.0.
   The Frobenius norm grows from 5.0 to 20.0 because the identity is not Hilbert-Schmidt, and boundedness depends on the largest singular value alone (03-b2b-operator.ipynb, Is the Operator Compact? A Numerical Check).

5. It returns a distribution over inputs, as a conditional normalizing flow does.
   Every input on the line f + c has the same derivative, so a model that returns one input per observation picks one point on that line.
   The linear map, the MLP, and the invertible network return constants near 0, the conditional mean of a_0 given f', and their input errors of 4.68e-1 and 4.69e-1 match the 4.68e-1 of the true f with a_0 set to 0.
   The invertible network undoes its own forward pass to 9.1e-15 and still fits the inverse direction only to 1.28e-1, because a bijection cannot send a line of inputs to one point.
   The flow samples spread along the line with a standard deviation of a_0 of 0.559, against 0.577 for the uniform training distribution, and their range contains the true a_0 for 391 of 400 test functions (03-fe-inverse.ipynb, Conditional normalizing flows, and What the two tables show).
