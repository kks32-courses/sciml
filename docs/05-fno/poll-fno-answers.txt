Answers to poll-fno.txt, in order, with the slide or notebook section behind each.

1. The multiplier R(k), or its matrix of feature-channel weights.
   The Fourier waves are fixed by the domain, and the FFT computes the coefficients of each input.
   Training fits the weights that transform those coefficients, and the same weights are used for each input ("Waves, coefficients, and multipliers", "Multipliers from data").

2. The second derivative multiplies sin(8x) by -64, giving it a faster amplitude decay.
   A sine amplitude q_k satisfies dq_k/dt = -nu k^2 q_k, so its decay factor is exp(-nu k^2 t).
   The decay rate depends on k^2 and nu, while the initial amplitude sets the starting value.
   Each Fourier mode evolves independently for this constant-coefficient periodic heat equation ("The multipliers of heat flow", fno-spectral-methods.ipynb, Section 2).

3. i.
   The positive-frequency coefficient of sin(x) is -i/2, and that of cos(x) is 1/2.
   Multiplication by i gives i(-i/2) = 1/2, and the multiplier at negative frequency is its conjugate, -i, so the reconstructed field is real.
   Complex multipliers can change phase as well as amplitude (fno-spectral-methods.ipynb, Section 3, "Amplitude and phase", and Section 5, "Mix channels at each retained frequency").

4. Each retained Fourier coefficient depends on samples across the whole domain.
   A truncated Fourier multiplier defines a convolution whose spatial kernel generally extends across the domain.
   Retaining fewer modes limits the kernel's spectral content while preserving this global spatial reach.
   The pointwise matrix W mixes channels at the same position ("The kernel of a Fourier layer", "One FNO block").

5. Multiplication by the spatially varying diffusivity couples modes even though L is linear in u.
   The flux is (1 + 0.5 cos(x)) 2 cos(2x), which contains cosine modes 1, 2, and 3.
   Differentiating gives -0.5 sin(x) - 4 sin(2x) - 1.5 sin(3x).
   The diffusivity is prescribed independently of u, so L remains linear.
   Its Fourier matrix is tridiagonal for this coefficient (fno-spectral-methods.ipynb, Section 3, "When independent multipliers are insufficient").

6. Feature channels at the same frequency.
   At each k, R_l(k) multiplies the vector of feature coefficients at that k.
   This spectral multiplication preserves the frequency index while mixing channels.
   Pointwise nonlinear activations can then generate additional frequencies ("Channels", "One FNO block").

7. A constant component and a second harmonic.
   The identity sin(x)^2 = 1/2 - cos(2x)/2 gives a mean of 1/2 and a mode-2 cosine coefficient of -1/2.
   The activation creates both components from a feature containing only mode 1.
   Subsequent spectral layers can apply learned weights to the new components when those modes are retained ("Squaring a wave", fno-spectral-methods.ipynb, Section 5).

8. The model can be evaluated on the finer grid, with accuracy depending on resolved scales and aliasing.
   Over the same physical domain, each retained Fourier index corresponds to the same physical wavenumber on both grids.
   The retained weight matrices can therefore be reused, but finer sampling adds no training information about unresolved physics.
   Nonlinear aliasing can also change with the evaluation grid, so accuracy needs a separate check ("Finer grids", "FNO properties", fno-spectral-methods.ipynb, Section 6).
